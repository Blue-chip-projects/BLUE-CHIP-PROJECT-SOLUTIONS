Make flow visible

Takt planning focuses attention on production rhythm, locations and trade sequence rather than viewing activities only as isolated bars on a timeline. Jenasaro provides planning surfaces for structuring work by area and process so teams can see how production is intended to move through the project.

This is particularly useful for repetitive or location-based work where reliable handovers between teams matter as much as individual activity durations.

ILLUSTRATIVE PROJECT SCENARIO

Example: coordinate services through three floors

  1. Define work areas and agree the trade sequence for each floor.
  2. Review the crews and working periods needed for each step.
  3. Check the handover conditions before releasing the next trade into an area.
  4. Review missed commitments and constraints before adjusting the sequence or team allocation.

Decision to make: Is each handover ready, and can the available crews sustain the intended rhythm?

Questions from project teams

When is takt planning useful?

It is useful when trades repeat a sequence through locations, such as floors, rooms or construction zones. The sequence and rhythm must be reviewed against quantities, available crews and access.

How does takt planning relate to the Gantt schedule?

Takt planning helps the team discuss location flow and trade handovers. The schedule supplies wider dependency and milestone context, while daily commitments show whether the intended work is being delivered.

Does software automatically guarantee a balanced takt plan?

No. Planners and trade teams must validate the chosen rhythm, production assumptions, constraints and resources. Jenasaro provides a shared environment for that review.
